Voltly EV Companion

Voltly is a personal dashboard for smarter EV travel. It combines a battery overview, a trip planner with charging station search, a saved-trip library, journey analysis, and vehicle settings in one dark, glanceable interface. I designed and developed it, and explored a separate UI concept in Figma Make.

It is a lightweight static web app. The data you enter is saved in your browser, and every figure is an estimate rather than live vehicle data.

Problem

EV users need convenient access to battery information, estimated range, and charging locations. Today these needs are often spread across different screens and apps.

Objective

Bring essential EV information and charging discovery into one intuitive interface that a driver can scan quickly.

What I implemented

  • Dashboard with a battery level you update yourself and an estimated range
  • Trip planner that calculates a route from place names or a typed distance
  • Charging station search near the start, along the road, and at the destination, shown on a map
  • Saved trips with planned and travelled status, CSV export, and a journey analysis view
  • Vehicle settings for range, consumption, and battery capacity

Design decisions

One task per tab

Navigation is split into Home, Planner, Saved Trips, Analysis, and Vehicle, so each screen has a single job.

Honest labels

The interface says the battery is manually updated, station data may be incomplete, and live availability is not guaranteed.

Shortcuts on Home

Quick actions jump straight to planning a journey, finding charging, reusing saved trips, or editing the vehicle.

Visual language

Palette

  • Night #090e0b
  • Panel #141d17
  • Voltage #caff4e

Principles

A dark interface for glanceable use. The lime accent is kept for primary actions and live values, and large numerals make battery and range readable at a glance.

Layout

A left sidebar holds navigation and a persistent battery card, so the charge level stays visible on every screen.

Honest scope

  • Voltly is a front-end web project. The repository holds static files only: HTML, CSS, two JavaScript files, a manifest, and an icon. Data such as battery level and vehicle settings is saved in the browser.
  • It is not connected to a real vehicle. The battery level is entered by the user, and range and energy figures are estimates, not live telemetry.
  • Charging station data may be incomplete, and the app does not show live availability.
  • The Figma Make piece is a UI concept exploration, separate from the coded site.
  • My vehicle design experience is concept-level. I have not shipped production automotive UI.

Why a static front end

The goal was a fast, understandable interface. Static files keep hosting simple and let me iterate on the design directly.

Estimate logic

Estimated range applies the battery percentage to the full-charge range in Vehicle settings. At 88% of a 300 km range, the dashboard shows 264 km.

Future direction

A real product would add a data layer: vehicle telemetry, a charging network feed, and accounts. That is the next step, not something I have built.

Design process

The steps I followed on Voltly, from problem to live site.

  1. Define the problem

    Identified the three things an EV driver checks most: battery level, range, and where to charge.

  2. Explore a concept in Figma Make

    Generated and refined a UI concept to compare layout ideas quickly before committing to code.

  3. Decide what belongs on one screen

    Kept battery, range, and charging discovery together so a driver does not switch between apps.

  4. Build in HTML, CSS, and JavaScript

    Turned the direction into a working site with maps and route planning, then split logic into script files.

  5. Publish and iterate

    Pushed to GitHub and deployed on Vercel so anyone can open the live version.
